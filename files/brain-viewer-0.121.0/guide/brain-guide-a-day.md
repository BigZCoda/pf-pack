# A day with your brain

What a normal day looks like, and what to say to Claude at each point. The lines in bold are examples: say them in your own words, and Claude understands.

[Back to the start](/reader?path=skills%2Fbrain-viewer%2Fguide%2Fbrain-guide-start-here.md)

## In the morning

Open a terminal in your brain folder and type `claude`. Claude reads what happened in your last session and what is open on your task lists, then types a short recap: where things stand, what is waiting on you, and anything you asked to be reminded of.

Open the viewer (say **start the viewer** if it isn't running) and look at Today. **Up first** holds the one to three things to do or answer now. Below it are what is due today and what is new since you last looked. Everything else of yours is folded under **Backlog**.

Things to say:

- **What's on my plate today?**
- **Remind me every session to check the event budget.** It goes into your standing reminders, which every recap repeats.
- **I'm meeting the venue manager at 2. What should I know?** Claude answers from that person's card and your past calls with them.

## On a call

Take part as usual. If your company allows it, have the meeting tool make a transcript (Google Meet, Zoom and Teams can all make one). Your brain is built from what was actually said, so the transcript matters more than your notes.

## After the call

Save the transcript as a text file in your brain's `transcripts` folder. Use the full transcript, not the short notes some tools make beside it. Then say:

- **Process the newest transcript.** Claude writes a summary, the decisions, the action items with their owners, and what the call says about the people on it, and updates their cards. Nothing from it leaves your computer.
- **Put my action items from that call on my task list.**
- **What did we decide about the vendor?** Claude answers from the transcript itself.

## Working on a task

On Today, each task has its buttons: **done** when it's finished, **hold** to put it off for a few days, and **note** for a line you want to keep with it. A question has a button for each answer, or a box to write your own. Every button you press is written into your brain right away.

When you are given an assignment, say **start a project** followed by its name. Claude asks for the assignment, then walks you through four steps, each ending in a file: what the project is for, who you need to talk to, the limits you have to work within, and the tools to build it with. The conversations you plan become tasks, and the questions for the people involved show on the Projects page.

While you work:

- **Add a task: send the vendor list to the events team by Friday.**
- **What's still open on the event project?**
- **I just learned the venue needs the final count a week ahead. Add it to the venue manager's card.**
- **That was wrong: always give me the next step first. Remember it.** Claude writes the correction into your brain's rules with the date, and every later session follows it.

## At the end of the day

Say:

- **I'm done for today.** Claude writes down what happened and what is still open, so tomorrow's session starts where this one stopped.
- **Commit and push.** This saves your brain to its backup. Do it at the end of every session.

## Once a week

- **Check for updates.** Claude shows what is new from ProspectForge and installs it only after your yes. When a newer viewer is ready, Today also says so in one line.
- Fill in the check-ins ProspectForge sends you as links. They are the one part of your work that goes to ProspectForge, and you write them yourself.
