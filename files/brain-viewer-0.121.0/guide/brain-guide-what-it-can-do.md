# What your brain can do

A short presentation in eight slides. Each one opens with its point. Everything in the first seven works in your brain today. The last one says where this is going and is marked as not part of your setup yet.

[Back to the start](/reader?path=skills%2Fbrain-viewer%2Fguide%2Fbrain-guide-start-here.md)

---

## 1. Your work, remembered

**A private brain on your own computer, and a viewer that runs your day.**

Most of what you learn at work lives in your head, in meetings and in inboxes, and some of it is lost every time a conversation ends. Your brain keeps it in one place you own: your calls, the people you work with, your projects and what is open on them. An assistant reads and writes it with you, so every session starts already caught up.

---

## 2. A folder you own, with an assistant inside

**Plain text files on your computer, kept alive by the way you work.**

- The brain is a folder of plain text files. You can open any of them with any text editor.
- Your own Claude reads and writes the folder under written rules, and logs every change with the time.
- It grows by routine: each day you work from inside it, so the brain of next month knows more than the brain of today, and nothing old is written over.

The idea is old. In 1945 Vannevar Bush imagined the memex, a private desk-sized store of all a person's records, read through screens and linked by trails, meant to extend memory. Niklas Luhmann kept a box of linked index cards for decades and described it as a partner he talked with. Your brain is in that line, with one difference: there is an assistant inside it that can act.

---

## 3. The viewer: where you work, not just where you read

**Your brain as pages, and every page lets you act.**

- **Today:** what is yours to do and answer, in one order, each with buttons to mark it done, hold it or add a note.
- **Projects:** each project's tasks and questions.
- **People:** a card for each person you work with, with what is open and your calls with them.
- **Threads:** one page for each piece of work, with where it stands.
- **Reader** and **Files:** read and edit any file in your brain.

Every button you press in the viewer is a change in your brain, logged like any other. The viewer moves nothing on its own: it shows you the state of your work and waits for you.

---

## 4. Calls become memory

**Every call becomes a record you can search and build on.**

Save a call's transcript in your brain and say **process the newest transcript**. You get:

- a summary and the key decisions
- the action items, each with an owner and a date
- what the call says about the people on it, folded into their cards

Afterward, ask **what did we decide about the venue?** and Claude answers from what was actually said.

---

## 5. Work you can act on

**Every project is a list of tasks and questions, and Today puts yours in one order.**

- Tasks say who they are for and when they are due. Questions name who should answer.
- Today shows what is up first, what is due and what is new, and folds the rest away.
- **Start a project** walks you through four steps, each ending in a file: what the project is for, who to talk to, the limits you work within, and the tools to build it with.

---

## 6. Ask in plain words

**You talk to your brain the way you'd talk to a colleague.**

- **What's on my plate today?**
- **What should I know before my call with the venue manager?**
- **survey my files** counts what you have elsewhere on your computer without opening anything, and **ingest the next type** brings one kind of it in after your yes.
- When you correct Claude, it writes the correction into the brain's rules with the date, and every later session follows it.

---

## 7. Yours, and it stays yours

**Everything in your brain stays on your computer.**

- Your brain sends nothing to ProspectForge, and ProspectForge has no access to it.
- What crosses the edge does so on purpose: what Claude reads to answer you goes to Anthropic, the company that runs Claude; **commit and push** saves your backup to your own private repository; you fill in your check-ins yourself.
- Updates arrive only after your yes. An update replaces the viewer's program whole, and what you set up yourself (your settings and your own pages) lives apart and is never touched.

---

## 8. Where this is going (not part of your setup yet)

**From one person's brain to a team that shares what it chooses.**

The direction, said here so you know it, and not something your brain does today:

- sharing chosen pieces of your work with teammates, each piece checked before it leaves your brain and again when it arrives;
- a team or company brain that people's brains contribute to through those checks, run by the company itself;
- drawings of how work moves through a team, kept beside the work and reading their state from it.

Your brain comes first. Everything after it is built on top of a brain that stays yours.
