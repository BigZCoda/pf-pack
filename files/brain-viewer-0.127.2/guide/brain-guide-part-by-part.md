# How your brain holds your work

What each part of your brain is, why it is held that way, and how the parts fit together.

[Back to the start](/reader?path=skills%2Fbrain-viewer%2Fguide%2Fbrain-guide-start-here.md)

## Whole parts that fit together

Each part of your brain is whole on its own: a project has its own folder and list, a person has one card, a thread is one file about one matter. Each part also fits into the larger brain, and a larger part can hold smaller ones (a project can hold other projects, a thread can be a piece of a bigger thread). The brain calls a part like this a holon: whole by itself, and a piece of something bigger.

Every fact has one home, and everything else links to it. That is why Claude can trust what it reads, and why nothing in your brain has to be typed twice.

A small part of the brain is fixed so the rest can stay reliable: your task lists are changed only through the task tool, every change is written to the brain's log with its time, and **today** puts your work in one order. Everything above that is yours to arrange: your projects, your threads, your drawings and your own pages in the viewer.

## Projects: tasks and questions

A project is a folder with one list. The list holds only two kinds of item:

- **a task**: one thing to do, owned by one person, with a due day when it has one;
- **a question**: a decision put to someone, with a press for each answer.

Everything you owe and everything someone owes you comes down to one of the two, so nothing gets lost between them. Your brain starts with one project, **Getting started**, which holds your first-week list. The **projects** page shows one project at a time, its items grouped under headings, and you can answer, tick off or reopen any of them there.

## Threads: one matter across many calls

Work at an employer rarely fits inside one call or one project. A thread is one file about one matter: a process you are fixing, a working relationship, something being launched. It says where the matter stands, what was decided, what you learned and where it came from, and it gathers every item about that matter from all your lists.

The **threads** page lists them. A thread's own page puts its open questions first, then where it stands, how it connects to other work, what was built for it and who is involved. Setup starts your first thread from what you said you are working on.

A thread is the memory of a matter. When you prepare for the next call about it, the prep starts from the thread, so you walk in knowing where things were left.

## People cards

Each person you work with has one card: who they are, their role, what they care about, what was said and what is open between you. Claude makes and updates the cards from your calls and from what you tell it. The **people** page shows every card with what is open that concerns each person.

Before you talk to someone, everything about them is in one place. Over time the cards add up to a picture of who does what at your employer.

## Starting a project

A project runs on a path: **assess, build, test, deliver, drive.** Assess is the part your brain walks through with you. The other four you work through with Claude in your own words; they have no fixed steps in a new brain yet.

Say **start a project** followed by its name. Claude asks for the assignment, then walks four steps, each ending in a file in the project's folder and a task on its list:

1. **The purpose.** What the project is for, questioned until it can be tested. Claude gives feedback, and any gap becomes a question for the people you will interview.
2. **Who to talk to.** The person it is for, the people who will use it every day, whoever runs the systems it touches, and someone to test it. Each conversation becomes a task. A role with no name yet becomes a task to find out who it is.
3. **The limits.** What you have to work within, in order of how much it matters. It is written again as each conversation's transcript comes in.
4. **The tools.** What to build it with, held back until the conversations you planned have happened.

Questions for the people involved go on the project's list, addressed to the person who can answer them. Ask **where am I on the returns project?** at any time.

The steps are a guiderail, not a gate. You can build, test or take a call in any order. A step run before its turn is marked as a draft and run again later.

## A way to think about it: the treasure map

Starting a project works like a map:

- **The quests** are the conversations you planned.
- **The characters** are the people and the roles they play.
- **The treasure** is what you collect: access, documents, examples, answers.
- **The finds** are the broken things you notice on the way. Write each one down as a task or a note and keep going. Do not chase it now; it comes back later as work of its own.

Across projects, the map adds up to a picture of who does what at your employer, kept in your people cards and threads. The pull to fix every broken thing you trip over is what most often derails a first project, and the map is a way to resist it.

The treasure map is a way to think. It has no page in the viewer yet.

## The Forge

The **forge** page holds drawings of how something works. Use it to draw how a process at your employer works before you build anything for it: each step, who is on it, what goes in and what comes out.

A drawing has three modes. **Read** shows it as drawn. **Live** shows each part against what really exists in your brain, so you can see what is built against what is only drawn. **Edit** changes it.

A **step board** draws a process as its steps, each step a file holding what happens there, its documents, its video and its form. The drawing is redrawn from those files every time it opens, so it never drifts from them. Say **make a step board for the onboarding process** and Claude sets one up with you.

Drawing first shows the gaps, and the people you have not talked to yet, before you build the wrong thing. A new brain has one example drawing to look at, and the page has a box to start your own.

## One level down: how each file is made

You do not need this to use your brain. It is here for when you want to see how it works underneath.

Every kind of file in your brain is made the same way each time, and the brain's **Anatomy** describes each kind:

- **its facets**: the named slots it carries, each with a meaning. A task's facets include its owner, its due day, its steps and its note; a thread's include its people and its status.
- **its contract**: how it is written so the viewer can read it. A task is one line on a list; a thread is one file with the same sections every time.
- **what writes it and what checks it**: one tool writes each kind, and a check reads it back. Your task lists are written only by the task tool, and the transcripts Claude reads in are checked after they are filed.

Because every file of one kind is made the same way, Claude and the viewer can rely on it instead of guessing from the wording.

To see it, press the **?** at the top right of a page, then **show its pieces on the page**. The page's pieces are outlined where they sit, each with what it is. It is written so far for the **projects** page, a thread's page and the parts every page carries; other pages say no pieces are written yet. The same panel opens every word the viewer uses, with a definition, and the manual.

## Sharing

Sharing is still being built. It turns on by invitation: someone you work with invites you, and you turn it on in **Settings** with your email and a code. Once it is ready, you can share a task or a thread with the people you work with. It shows on their **today**, and their replies come back to yours.

Nothing leaves your brain until you share it, and on some employers' computers sharing stays off.

## What stays on the computer you work on

Everything in your brain stays on the computer you work on. A few things cross its edge, each on purpose:

- **Claude.** To answer you, Claude sends what it reads to the company that runs it, under the account you sign in with.
- **Updates.** Checking for updates reads the list of new versions and sends nothing about you. Nothing installs without your yes.
- **The backup.** **Commit and push** saves your brain to its own repository, if it has one.
- **Sharing**, once it is on, and only what you share.

An update replaces the viewer's own folder, `skills/brain-viewer`, whole, so do not keep changes of yours there. What you set up yourself (your settings and your own pages) lives in the `viewer` folder, which no update touches.
