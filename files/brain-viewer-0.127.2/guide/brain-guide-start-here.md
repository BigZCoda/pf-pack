# Your brain: start here

This guide says how your brain works and how it helps you move forward in your work. It is short on purpose: read this page and the day with your brain first, and come back to the rest as you need it.

## What your brain is for

Your brain is one folder you own, on the computer you work on. Everything you learn about your work collects there: your calls, the people you work with, your projects, what was decided and what you found out on the way.

Claude reads all of it before it answers you. You never explain the same thing twice: tell it once who someone is or what a project is for, and every later session already knows.

Things go in rough. A half-formed note, a call transcript, a list of names you were given on your first day. You do not need to organize anything before it goes in. Over the weeks, you and Claude refine it into people cards, task lists and threads, and the brain of your third month knows what your first month taught it.

This matters most at the start of a placement, when most of what you need lives in other people's heads and in conversations that end. The brain is how you keep it, find it again and act on it.

The brain is the folder. The viewer is the window onto it: it draws your files as pages in your browser, and anything you do in it is written back into those files. Both run on the computer you work on.

## Two ways to work

**Claude in the terminal** is where you talk, think out loud and do the bigger work: reading in a call, starting a project, writing a document, working something out. Every day starts here.

**The viewer** is where you are guided. The **today** page says what is next, in one order, and every press you make there (done, hold, a note, an answer) is written into your files. The **notepad** at the bottom left of every page keeps a thought the moment you have it. The **?** at the top right of every page says what that page is and what each part of it does.

## Your first day

1. **Set it up.** Open a terminal in your brain folder and type `claude`. Say **set up my brain**. Claude asks your name, what you are working on and how you like information delivered. What you are working on becomes your first thread, and your first-week list goes onto the **Getting started** project. It takes about five minutes.
2. **Open the viewer.** Go to http://127.0.0.1:8765 in your browser. It opens on **today**, with your first-week list on it. If the page does not answer, say **start the viewer** to Claude.
3. **Read a day with your brain**, then try one thing from it.

## What a new brain does differently at the start

- **You type first.** Claude cannot speak before you do. Type anything, or `/start-session`, and it gives you the rundown: where things stand, what is waiting on you, and anything you asked to be reminded of.
- **The viewer keeps running after you close Claude,** so its pages keep working. To close it, open **Settings** and press **Stop the viewer** at the foot of the page, then **Yes, stop it**. The next time you start Claude in your brain, it comes back on its own.
- **What you press in the viewer reaches Claude when you next start a session,** not within the minute. Done and hold change your task list at once, so **today** shows them right away. Your answers and notes wait, and Claude reads them first thing in your next session.
- **Today is quiet at first.** It shows no calls, because nothing in a new brain reads your calendar yet. Up first is chosen by the page from your own lists. It fills in as you add work: every task, call and person you bring in gives it more to put in order.

## When something does not work

Tell Claude what you saw and what you typed. It can read the brain's own instructions and often fix the problem. If it cannot, tell the people who set up your brain.
