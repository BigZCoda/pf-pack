what this is, and how to use it

A place to design a system before it exists. A cell is one system, drawn as a circle: the gold ring around it is the band, what people see, and its segments are the screens, forms and messages; inside the ring is the core, what runs, with minds at the middle; what the cell makes leaves through its output, a section of the shell on the outside of the wall. A part is a typed piece inside a cell. A line is information moving from one part to another, in a direction, with a label saying what moves; between two cells it runs rim to rim. Every cell carries a stage: concept, prototype, built, stable.

Add a cell: double-click empty canvas, or drag Cell from the palette. Add a part: drag a tile from the palette onto a cell; people-facing parts land on the band, everything else in the core. Drop on empty canvas for a free part.

Connect: rest the pointer on a part or a cell, drag from one of the four points on its rim to another, then type what moves. While a line is being drawn, a letter puts a new part on it: h hook s step a agent d store u screen. (An output is not one of them: it is a section of a cell's shell, and o opens its shelf.)

The output: every cell can carry one output — a section of its shell on the outside of the wall, in its own color, which slides around the ring so it can face whatever comes next. Double-click it (or press o with the cell selected) for its shelf: loose is every field the cell makes, packaged is what has been named and is ready to ship. Hold Alt and click the fields you want, name what they make together, and the package appears on the ring just outside the mouth; drag it onto another cell and the line is drawn for you. Where the cell is bound to something real, the loose fields are read from that thing's own records rather than typed. A store dragged against the wall is kept there as a round blob, and a click opens the table, file or page behind it.

An adapter: a boundary the brain calls across — an API, an export, a hook. On a drawing it is one symbol with its counts underneath and nothing fanned out beside it: how many routes the other side has, how many this token may call, how many nothing in the brain calls yet. A small clock means the inventory it was drawn from is more than a week old. Double-click it and its sheet opens — a page of its own where nothing hand-drawn competes: the calling skills named down the left, one port per route down the middle grouped by resource and, inside a group, by who may call it (the personal token first, the admin key next, the ones nothing calls last), and the app's stores named down the right, with a short cable from each port to what calls it and to what it reaches. Solid means the token the brain holds may call it, dim means it needs the admin key, hollow means the route exists and nothing in the brain calls it, red means a skill calls a path the app has never listed, a red ring means a probe came back an error, and a dotted port is one written by hand in the registry rather than read from the app. Press a port for its card: the purpose, the callers as files you can open, when it last ran, the mirror, the health. An adapter dropped inside a cell stays small: three counted arcs on its rim — personal, admin, hollow — and a cable to each part of that cell that calls it. None of it is in the drawing: it is read from the boundary's own catalog every time, plus any hand-written groups in skills/brain-viewer/adapters.json, and the same answer is written as pf-app-holon/pf-app-adapter-<name>.md for an agent to grep.

Reading it from far out: with the whole drawing on screen only the names are drawn — the cells, the parts, the stores at the wall and the packages on the rings — so the shape of the system reads at a glance. The quieter words (what moves along a line, where it came from, the mouth's own word) come back when you rest the pointer on a line or on either thing it joins, and they are all drawn once you zoom past 60%.

Move things: a part dragged inside its cell stays exactly where you drop it, and one on the gold ring slides along the ring; drag a cell and everything inside travels with it. A part you have never moved is arranged by the cell. The inspector can hand a part back to the arrangement.

Change things: click to select, shift-click or shift-drag for several, hold S and drag to scale the selection (S is scale whenever no line is being dragged; while one is, it puts a step on the line), double-click text to edit it in place, Delete asks before removing. The inspector on the right edits the name, the type, the doc, and a cell's stage and its output. Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+S saves. Nothing is saved until you press Save. The file keeps Obsidian's boxes; the circles are how this page reads them.
